About rekt.

rekt is a trading game. You start with $10,000 of play money and trade real prices: over 400 coins live from Coinbase, and about 175 stocks and ETFs live from Kraken. Nothing you do here touches real money, and there's nothing to sign up for.

Trade

Open a long (you win if the price goes up) or a short (you win if it goes down). Leverage multiplies the bet: at 10×, $1,000 of margin controls a $10,000 position, so a 1% move is a 10% win or loss on your margin.

If the price moves far enough against you, the position is liquidated: the exchange closes it and your margin is gone. At 10× that takes about a 9.5% move; at 100×, half a percent. The liquidation price is shown before you open, on the chart, and on every position.

Like a real exchange, there's a 0.05% fee on the position's full size, when you open and when you close. At high leverage that adds up fast. Positions stay open when you close the tab; when you come back, the game checks Coinbase's price history to see whether any got liquidated while you were gone.

Stocks

Stock prices come from xStocks on Kraken: tokens that each track one share of a real stock or ETF (AAPLx tracks Apple, SPYx the S&P 500). Their prices are public and free, which live stock exchange prices aren't. The game uses the middle of the best bid and ask, the mark price, because the tokens trade less often than their quotes move.

xStocks trade around the clock, but when the US stock market is closed (evenings, nights and weekends) there's little to move them, so their prices drift slowly and can differ a little from the real share's. The game says when the market is closed. During market hours they follow the shares closely.

History

A hundred years of the US stock market on one chart, with every crash of 20% or more in red, found from the data itself. Tap one and it plays as a film: your money on screen, real headlines at their real dates, and two moments where you decide whether to sell, hold or buy more. At the end you're compared with three plain strategies: holding, selling at the bottom and buying at the bottom.

The data is the daily return of the whole US stock market since July 1926, dividends reinvested: every US company listed on the NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ, weighted by size. It tracks the S&P 500 closely (the S&P 500's own daily numbers are licensed and can't be shown here). The market only has closing prices, so the film's candles are built from closes: nothing between them is invented. "Adjust for inflation" uses the US consumer price index, month by month.

Up or Down

Pick a coin, a stake and a clock (30 seconds, a minute or five), and call whether the price will be higher or lower when the time runs out. Right: your stake comes back doubled. Wrong: it's gone. Exactly the same price: you get your stake back.

The fine print

This is a game, not financial advice, and not a simulation you should base real trades on. Real exchanges have order books, funding rates, slippage and tiered margin, which the game leaves out. If you lose all your play money, you can get bailed out with a fresh $10,000, as many times as you like. Your bailouts are counted, though.

Prices come from the public market data of Coinbase Exchange (crypto) and Kraken (xStocks). This site isn't affiliated with or endorsed by either, or by any company whose stock appears in it.
